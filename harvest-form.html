HARVEST Form

Celebrating Natural Form, Culture and Creative Expression

Our Artistic Journey

HARVEST Form celebrates the unique shapes, textures and irregularities of pumpkins and gourds while connecting artistic expression with science, gardening, history and cultural traditions.

Toddlers and PreCASA children begin by painting facial features on small pumpkins, developing recognition of form and early representational skills.

CASA students expand their creativity through painted pumpkin designs, while Grades 1–3 explore three-dimensional form by carving pumpkins grown in the school garden.

In Grades 4–6, students progress to miniature paintings on gourds, considering how imagery and composition relate to the natural shape of their chosen surface.

By Grades 7 and 8, the work becomes increasingly personal and refined, integrating symbolism, artistic intention and technical skill to create meaningful works that celebrate the relationship between nature, culture and art.

Toddlers

Discovering a Face

Toddlers paint miniature pumpkins, exploring eyes, a nose, a mouth and ears through simple painted or drawn marks. Adults guide children in recognising and naming facial features while encouraging independent expression.

The focus is on facial recognition, purposeful marks and the connection between movement and its visible result. Each child's interpretation is valued.

Precasa

Bringing a Face Together

PreCASA children paint miniature pumpkins, exploring the placement of eyes, a nose, a mouth and ears. They begin to understand how facial features relate to one another while working on a curved surface.

The focus is on body awareness, spatial relationships and purposeful placement. Each child's individual expression is encouraged, with guidance suited to their readiness.

CASA 1

Small Pumpkin with Personality

CASA students paint pumpkins as seasonal jack-o’-lanterns or faces, using their science observations of the stem, ridges, shape and surface to guide their artwork.

The focus is on shape recognition, brush control and creating recognisable facial features. Students explore how the pumpkin’s natural form adds personality while practising controlled painting on a curved surface.

CASA 2

Medium Pumpkin with Personality

CASA students paint medium pumpkins as seasonal jack-o’-lanterns or faces, using science observations of the stem, ridges, shape and surface to guide their artwork.

The focus is on proportion, expression and placement of facial features. Students explore how size and positioning create personality while practising controlled painting on a curved surface.

CASA 3

Large Pumpkin with Personality

CASA students paint large pumpkins as seasonal jack-o’-lanterns or faces, using science observations of the stem, ridges, shape and surface to guide their artwork.

The focus is on composition, independent planning and purposeful design. Students explore how the pumpkin’s natural form creates personality while practising controlled painting on a curved surface.

GRADE 1

From Garden to Lantern

Students transform pumpkins into jack-o’-lanterns or faces by removing their contents and carving facial features with pumpkin cutters under adult guidance.

The focus is on sequencing, supported tool use and purposeful placement. Students explore the flesh, seeds and fibrous interior before marking and carving their pumpkin faces.

GRADE 2

From Garden to Lantern

Students transform pumpkins into jack-o’-lanterns or faces by removing their contents and carving facial features with pumpkin cutters under adult supervision.

The focus is on translating shapes into openings and controlled tool use. Students explore the flesh, seeds and fibrous interior before planning and carving their pumpkin faces.

GRADE 3

From Garden to Lantern

Students transform pumpkins into jack-o’-lanterns or faces by removing their contents and carving facial features with pumpkin cutters under adult supervision.

The focus is on positive and negative space, exploring how carved openings and the pumpkin surface create expression and transmit light. Students observe the flesh, seeds and fibrous interior before planning their designs.

GRADE 4

A Story in Miniature

Students select a gourd and create a miniature painting with personal meaning, suited to its shape and natural character.

The focus is on observation, image placement and the relationship between image and form. Students consider how lines and colours complement the gourd’s curves, ridges and natural markings.

GRADE 5

Painting Beyond the Flat Surface

Students select a gourd and create a miniature painting with personal meaning, suited to its proportions and natural character.

The focus is on proportion, composition and image placement on a curved surface. Students consider how scale, lines and colours complement the gourd’s contours, ridges and natural markings.

GRADE 6

Harmony in Miniature

Students select a gourd and create a miniature painting with personal meaning, suited to its proportions and natural character.

The focus is on detail, colour and visual unity. Students consider how their image, colours and details complement the gourd’s curves, ridges and natural markings.

GRADE 7

Symbols of Self

Students select a gourd and create a miniature painting with personal meaning, suited to its proportions and natural character.

The focus is on personal symbolism and artistic intention. Students use imagery, colour and detail to express personal ideas, explaining how their artwork’s meaning relates to the chosen gourd.

GRADE 8

Form, Meaning and Expression

Students select a gourd and create a miniature painting with personal meaning, suited to its proportions and natural character.

The focus is on integrating meaning, form and technical refinement. Students use imagery, composition and symbolism to express their ideas, explaining how the artwork’s message relates to the chosen gourd.
